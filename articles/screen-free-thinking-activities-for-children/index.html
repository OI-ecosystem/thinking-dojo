Seven screen-free thinking activities for children aged 7 to 13

None of these needs a screen, a worksheet or any preparation. Each one practises a single thinking skill and takes a few minutes of a day you were already having.

The Thinking Dojo · 15 November 2026

1. The walk-home report · Notice

On any walk, each of you names one thing you noticed that the other might have missed. You take a turn as well.

2. The Sunday pattern · Pattern

At Sunday dinner, ask what usually happens on the mornings that go well. Then ask what usually happens when everyone is in a rush. Let the children answer before the adults do.

3. The three-option hour · Choice

Give your child a free hour. Before they start, they name three things they could do with it and what might happen with each. Then they choose.

4. First-step cooking · Plan

Your child plans a simple meal. Ask two things: what is the goal, and what do you do first? Then cook it their way.

5. One small step · Act

Pick something they have been putting off, such as a messy desk or a thank-you note. Ask “what is one small step?” and do only that step today.

6. The replay · Review

After a board game or a match, everyone answers one question: what would you do differently next time? Winners answer too.

7. The dinner debate · Independent thinking

Choose a small question. Should homework exist? Is it ever right to break a promise? Each child says what they think and why. The adults give their views last, so nobody is guessing the right answer.

Making it a habit

The hard part is remembering to ask. That is what the Dojo’s thinking cards are for: a pack for each skill, kept in a kitchen drawer, one card pulled at a time.
