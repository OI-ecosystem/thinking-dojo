Three questions for Anti-Bullying Week: notice, choose, act

Anti-Bullying Week runs from Monday 16 to Friday 20 November 2026, and this year’s theme is Break the Silence. Speaking up starts earlier than it looks, with noticing. Here are three questions for the week, five minutes each, for a classroom or a kitchen table.

The Thinking Dojo · 16 November 2026

What the week is

Anti-Bullying Week is run each November by the Anti-Bullying Alliance. The 2026 theme, Break the Silence, asks children and adults to start conversations about bullying, to speak up, to listen and to act together. The week opens with Odd Socks Day on Monday 16 November.

The Alliance publishes its own school resources, and those come first. You can find them on the Anti-Bullying Alliance website.

The Thinking Dojo is not part of the campaign and is not an anti-bullying programme. What follows is a short thinking routine that sits beside the week’s own materials and your school’s policy.

Why start with noticing

A child can’t speak up about something they haven’t noticed. And they can’t choose what to do about it until they can say what they saw.

So the order matters. Notice first, then choose, then act. These are three of the seven thinking skills the Dojo teaches, and each has one plain question.

Early in the week · Notice

White Belt · NoticeWhat did you notice today?

Ask it as it stands, then steer gently towards people. Who was on their own at break? Who went quiet? Who wasn’t in the game?

You are asking pupils to describe what they saw. Nobody has to be named and nobody is being accused. A second question helps them look closer:

White Belt · NoticeWhat might others have missed?

Midweek · Choose

Orange Belt · ChoiceWhat might happen if you choose this?

Use a made-up situation, so no real child is the subject. For example: someone in the class is left out of the same game every day.

Take three choices in turn and ask the question about each:

  • Say nothing.
  • Tell an adult.
  • Ask them to join in.

Let pupils follow each choice through. They will usually work out for themselves that saying nothing is also a choice, and that it has a result.

End of the week · Act

Blue Belt · ActWhat is one small step?

Small is the point. Sitting next to someone counts. So does asking “Are you all right?”

Telling an adult counts too. For many children it is the hardest step, and it is the one this year’s theme is about.

The five-minute routine

  1. Put one question on the board.
  2. One minute of silent thinking.
  3. Two minutes in pairs.
  4. Two minutes hearing from three or four pupils.

Nothing is written down and nothing is marked.

At home

Ask one question at dinner or in the car, then wait. If your child describes something that happened to them or to someone else, listen first. The urge to fix it straight away is strong, and it can end the conversation.

Thank them for telling you. Then, if it sounds like bullying, speak to the school.

If a child tells you something worrying

A question like this can bring out more than you expected. In school, follow your safeguarding and anti-bullying procedures and speak to your designated safeguarding lead. These questions support that process. They don’t replace it.

After the week

Noticing, choosing and acting are habits, so they need more than five days. The same questions work on any Monday of the year. There is a full week of them in five-minute thinking starters for the classroom.
