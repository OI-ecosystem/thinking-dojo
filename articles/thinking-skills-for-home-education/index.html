A simple thinking-skills routine for home education

You already have enough to plan. Thinking skills work better as a habit that sits inside the subjects you teach than as one more subject on the timetable.

The Thinking Dojo · 15 November 2026

Attach it to what you already do

You don’t need a new slot in the day. You need three places to put a question:

  • At breakfast: one card, one question.
  • Before a project: a planning question.
  • On Friday: a review question about the week.

A week that works

  • Monday. Look at the Thinking Progress Chart and decide which belt fits how your child is thinking now. You are observing, so there is nothing to test.
  • Every day. Pull one card from that belt’s pack. Ask it, then listen without correcting.
  • Midweek. Read one of the stories together.
  • Before any project. Ask “What is your goal?” and “What do you do first?”
  • Friday. Ask “What would you do differently next time?” about one thing from the week.

More than one child

Children of different ages can be on different belts at the same table. Ask each child the question from their own belt. Younger children usually spend longer on the early belts, which is how it is meant to work.

If you want guided sessions

The Coach Engine runs a 10–15 minute session of six questions at your child’s belt level. They write their thinking in their own words. It runs in a web browser on iPhone, iPad and desktop.

Keeping a record

If you keep a record of learning, the Coach Engine’s session summaries can be exported, and the Starter System includes a Progress Tracker.

What to start with

The Starter System has the paper core: the workbook, the story series, the printable thinking cards, the tracker and the chart. If you would rather try the idea first, print the seven free cards.
