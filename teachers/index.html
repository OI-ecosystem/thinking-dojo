For teachers

The Thinking Dojo in the classroom.

Seven thinking skills in a fixed order, each with questions pupils can answer out loud. It fits ages 7 to 13 and needs no marking.

What it gives a class

Most pupils are told to “think about it” without being shown the steps. The Dojo names seven steps and puts them in order: Notice, Pattern, Choice, Plan, Act, Review, and thinking independently.

The Plan, Act and Review steps line up closely with planning, monitoring and evaluating, which is how the Education Endowment Foundation describes metacognitive strategies. The EEF’s Toolkit puts the average impact of those approaches at eight months of additional progress, and notes that this can be difficult to achieve in practice. Read the EEF’s summary.

Six places a card fits into the day

  • During a story. Pause while you read and ask the whole class “What did you notice?” Each pupil writes an answer on a printed card.
  • As a starter. One question on the board, one minute to think, two minutes in pairs. Here is a full week of starters.
  • After a playground fall-out. Start with Notice. Each pupil describes what actually happened before anyone says what it means.
  • Before a project. “What is your goal?” and “What do you do first?”
  • Halfway through a task. “What is one small step?” for anyone who is stuck.
  • After a test or a piece of work. “What would you do differently next time?”

How it stays low-stakes

The Dojo has no scores, no tests and no pass mark. Pupils move up a belt when they use the next skill without prompting. The Thinking Progress Chart describes each belt in one plain sentence, so you can place a pupil by listening to them.

Where to start

Print the seven free cards and use one a day for a week. If it works for your class, the Workbook introduces all seven belts step by step, and the Starter System adds the story series, the printable card packs for every belt and the chart.

You can use your pack with your whole class and print as many cards as your pupils need.
